Three directions for Bamio

Phase A, brand exploration. Each direction is a different bet on what “premium” means for a short-form video tool. Pick one to carry into the design system, or tell me what to combine.

  • Phase A of M
  • Date 25 Sep 2026
  • Status Waiting for your review

What every direction shares

The directions differ in mood, color, type and mark. The strategy underneath them is the same.

What Bamio is

An AI video studio for vertical, short-form video. You bring an idea; Bamio writes the story, plans the shots, cuts the edit and renders it, and you direct every step.

The product runs idea to export in one place: story builder, storyboard, editor, an AI director that gives notes, templates, and rendering.

Who it is for

  • Solo creators posting daily who need volume without losing quality.
  • Brand and social teams who need on-brand shorts every week.
  • Small agencies producing short-form for several clients.

What the name gives us

Bam is impact: the clap of a slate, the first second that stops a thumb. The -io ending echoes studio, audio and radio, so the name sounds like a place where media gets made.

So the brand holds a tension on purpose: a hard start and an open finish, premium but lively. The ending can also read as tech (“.io”), so the brand leans on the studio meaning, not the developer one.

What the category looks like

Studied in the reference library: Runway, Framer and Linear all sit on near-black canvases with one restrained accent and a quiet interface. AI video tools add purple and blue glow on top.

Dark alone will not make Bamio recognisable. Ownership has to come from the accent, the type, the mark and the way Bamio talks.

Non-negotiables in every direction

  • Video sits on neutral darkThe editor, storyboard and preview use untinted dark greys so footage is judged accurately. The brand shows up in the accent, type and marketing, never as a tint around video.
  • 9:16 is the native canvasThe vertical frame is the unit of the product and a recurring brand motif.
  • One accent, lockedA single brand color with a fixed job. Success, warning and error get their own colors.
  • No AI clichésNo purple glow, no sparkle icon for “AI”, no floating gradient blobs. AI is shown through what it does.
  • Plain, specific copySentence case, concrete verbs, no em dashes, no “elevate” or “unleash”.
  • Accessible by defaultText and controls meet WCAG AA in the editor and on marketing pages.

Recommendation

Take One, with Hook’s warmth in the voice

Take One gives Bamio the most premium and most coherent system. Its voice can drift cold, so borrow Hook’s plain, friendly microcopy to keep the brand lively.

  • One world from homepage to editor. The brand and the editing surface are both dark, so the product feels like the brand. Hook and Director’s Cut switch from light marketing to a dark editor.
  • The accent has a real job. Tally means “live”, which the product needs for the playhead, rendering and the primary action. It passes on dark at 6.0:1. Director’s Cut’s accent fails in the editor.
  • The mark says the name. The hinged b is the clap of the slate, it survives at 16px, and nobody else in the category owns it.

Watch-outs if we go this way

  • Differentiate through the condensed type, the tally color and film grammar, not through darkness.
  • Keep errors visually separate from tally: a crimson plus an icon, never color alone.
  • Test the mark with real users at 16px and 32px before Phase B locks it.
