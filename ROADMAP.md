# Bamio roadmap: from a working v0.2 to the best clipping tool there is

Written 2026-10-04 from a full read of the code at commit `3153893` (28 commits since 2026-09-27, about 20,000 lines in `web/src` and `web/workers`, 173 unit tests, 28 end-to-end tests) and of `DESIGN_STATUS.md`. It lists everything between today's app and a product that is fully working for paying strangers, then everything between that and the best tool of its kind. Each item names the files it touches. Sizes are rough: S under a day, M a few days, L a week or more.

## 1. Where Bamio stands

Bamio already does the hard, differentiating things well:

- **Transcription in every language, on the device, with a time for every word.** Parakeet, Omnilingual and Whisper through sherpa-onnx, language detection by vote, code-mixed repair, script repair, loudness per second. Measured against YouTube's own timings (97% of words within 0.25 s). No competitor does this on CPU for free; most pay per minute for a cloud ASR and get worse timing.
- **Live streams followed while they are edited** (growing HLS, captions in pieces, exports mid-stream, one MP4 at the end). Very few tools do this at all.
- **A production-grade core for a one-person project:** Postgres as data store and durable job queue (leases, retries, priority, cancel, NOTIFY), a storage interface for local disk and S3/R2, idempotent job handlers, zod on every stored document and every model answer, signed-in and cross-site checks on every route, SSRF checks on links, an outbox for emails, a Stripe integration that re-reads state so it works without webhooks, an admin panel, SEO done properly, a design system with WCAG AA and a measured responsive pass.
- **Preview equals export** in every script, with fonts chosen per character the same way in the browser and in libass.

What is missing falls into three groups: things that make it **not yet safe to sell to strangers** (section 2), **architecture that will not carry growth** (section 3), and **features the leaders in this category have that Bamio sells as "coming soon"** (section 4): 41 feature lines in `web/src/lib/billing/plans.ts` carry `soon: true`.

## 2. Blockers: what "fully working" still needs (P0)

Do these before spending on marketing. Each one either loses users at the door, loses data or money, or creates legal exposure.

### 2.1 The funnel has a paywall before any value

- **No free tier and no trial.** `assertCanProcess` (`web/src/lib/server/billing.ts`) answers 402 `plan_required` to any import without a plan. The landing hero says "Get clips", sends the visitor through sign-in, and then stops them at "Choose a plan to import videos." Nobody pays $12 for a tool they have not seen work. Every leader in this category lets people make a few clips free (watermarked and limited). Add one of: a free plan (for example 30 minutes a month, 720p or watermarked, 5 projects), or a 7-day trial on Stripe (`trial_period_days`, with `payment_method_collection: if_required`), or 2 free imports per account. Watermarking needs an `overlay` step in `renderFilter` (`web/src/lib/clips/render.ts`) and a `watermark` flag on the allowance. **L**
- **No demo for a new account.** An empty `/projects` page after sign-up. Seed a sample project (a short CC-licensed talk) on first sign-in, or let the landing demo's link open a shared read-only project. **M**
- **No welcome email** (Clerk sends its own; Bamio's would carry "paste your first link"). Needs the Clerk webhook below. **S**
- **No product analytics.** There is no way to see where people drop (landing to sign-up to first import to first export to checkout). Add a privacy-friendly tool (Plausible or PostHog, self-hostable) with the handful of server-side events that matter: signed up, import started, import ready, export done, checkout started, paid. **S**
- **"Priority support" is sold but there is no support channel anywhere in the app** (no mailto, no contact page, no in-app feedback). Add a support address in the footer and account menu, a "Report a problem" link on failed imports that pre-fills the project id, and an inbox someone reads. **S**

### 2.2 Legal and account basics

- **`/terms` and `/privacy` do not exist** (noted in `DESIGN_STATUS.md`; Google's OAuth consent screen and Stripe both expect them). Also needed: a refund policy line, a DMCA/takedown page and address (the app downloads other people's videos on request), and an acceptable-use line matching the existing "only import videos you own" notice. Add them to `USE_CASES` or the sitemap and footer (`web/src/lib/site.ts`, `web/src/components/site/site-chrome.tsx`). **M**
- **Account deletion.** Deleting a Clerk account leaves projects, media, billing, usage, emails and referral rows behind, and a Stripe subscription that keeps charging. Add a Clerk webhook route (`user.deleted`, verified with the Svix signature, no session, like the Stripe webhook) that cancels the subscription, deletes the user's projects and media (`deleteProject` for each), and removes billing, usage, grants, referral and admin rows. Also a "Delete my account" button in `/profile` and a data export (projects JSON plus transcripts) for GDPR requests. **M**
- **Model attribution.** NVIDIA Parakeet is CC-BY-4.0 and `README.md` says "credit NVIDIA if you ship the app", but nothing in the UI does (`grep NVIDIA src/app src/components` finds nothing). Add a credits line to the footer or an `/about` page. **S**
- **Cookie notice** for the referral cookie (`/r/[code]`, 60 days) if EU visitors are expected: it is a first-party functional cookie, so a short note in the privacy policy covers it, but the policy has to exist. **S**

### 2.3 Operations: one laptop, one Droplet, no safety net

- **No CI.** There is no `.github/` folder. The 173 unit tests, the build and the e2e suite run only on the developer's Windows laptop, against Edge (`channel: "msedge"` in `web/playwright.config.ts`). The ffmpeg-static segfault was found in production because nothing ever ran the Docker image before deploy. Add a GitHub Actions workflow on every push: `npm run check`, `npm run build`, `docker build`, then the default e2e suite inside the built image against a Postgres service (Chromium in CI, `E2E_CHANNEL=chromium`; cache `.models` for the English model and Whisper; a Clerk development instance's keys as secrets). **M**
- **Deploys build on the production box.** `DEPLOY.md` says `git pull && docker compose up -d --build` on the Droplet: a 5 to 10 minute build competing for 2 vCPUs and 4 GB with running imports, then a restart that ends every followed stream ("restart" end reason) and interrupts exports. Build the image in CI, push it to GHCR with the commit SHA as its tag, and have the Droplet `docker compose pull && up -d`. Add a staging environment (a second small Droplet or a `staging.` host) that gets every merge to `main` first. **M**
- **Backups stay on the same disk.** A daily `pg_dump` to `/root/backups` on the Droplet; media is not backed up at all; DigitalOcean's Droplet backups were suggested, not confirmed on. A disk failure loses every customer's videos and the database. Ship dumps off-box nightly (R2 or S3, `rclone` or `pg_dump | aws s3 cp`), test a restore once, and either move media to R2 (section 3) or sync `/data/media` to R2 nightly. Longer term: a managed Postgres with point-in-time recovery. **S** for off-box dumps, **M** for media.
- **No error tracking, no alerting, no metrics.** Everything is `console.log` and `console.error` in `docker compose logs`. Nobody is told when `/api/health` turns 503, when a job fails for good, when YouTube's cookies expire (`explainYtdlpError` only logs a warning), or when the disk fills. Add Sentry (or GlitchTip, self-hosted) for exceptions in the web server and the worker, an uptime check on `/api/health` (Better Uptime, Healthchecks.io, or DigitalOcean's monitoring) with alerts, and a small metrics endpoint or scheduled job that emails or pings when: failed jobs in the last hour exceed N, oldest waiting job exceeds 10 minutes, free disk under 10 GB, the "not a bot" warning fires. **M**
- **Structured logs with a request id and a job id**, so a user's failed import can be found in the log by project id. A thin wrapper around `console` (`[bamio/...]` prefixes exist already) emitting JSON lines is enough. **S**
- **The web server and the CPU-bound worker share 2 vCPUs.** `compose.yaml` runs one `app` service with `BAMIO_WORKER` on, so transcription (which deliberately takes two thirds of the machine, `speechCpu` in `web/src/lib/server/transcribe.ts`) runs beside Next.js, Postgres and Caddy on a 2 vCPU / 4 GB Droplet. Pages will crawl during imports, and Omnilingual 1B (about 800 MB of model) plus Parakeet (480 MB) plus parallel decodes plus Next.js plus Postgres is at the edge of 4 GB (the swap file hides OOM kills as slowness). On the laptop that produced the speed figures (6 cores), Omnilingual runs at 2 to 4x real time; on 2 shared vCPUs expect roughly real time or slower, so an hour of Hindi takes an hour or more. Split the services (`BAMIO_WORKER=off` on `app`, a `worker` service from the same image with `deploy.resources.limits.cpus`), and size the worker for the load: 4 vCPU / 8 GB at least, or a dedicated CPU Droplet. Measure with `E2E_PERF=1` on the server, not the laptop. **S** to split, money to size.
- **Capacity limits are not enforced per user.** Projects are capped (50/150/400) but not bytes: a 3-hour 1080p import is 5 to 8 GB, a 12-hour followed stream about 32 GB, and the production disk is 77 GB. `assertDiskSpace` refuses work when the disk is nearly full, which means one user's long stream blocks everyone's imports. Add a per-plan storage quota (bytes used per user, summed from `source`, `exports` and `live` sizes on the project), a retention policy (delete source media of projects untouched for N days, keep clips and exports, or offer "archive"), and the orphan and scratch sweepers below. **M**

### 2.4 Hygiene jobs that do not exist yet

- **Scratch folders leak on failure.** `runImport` (`web/src/lib/server/jobs.ts`) removes `work.dir` only on success; a failed or cancelled import, a crashed worker or a `TookTooLong` leaves gigabytes under `BAMIO_WORK_DIR`. Add a sweeper (a periodic task beside `pruneJobs` in `worker.ts`) that deletes scratch folders of projects with no running job that are older than a few hours. **S**
- **Orphaned media.** `deleteProject` (`web/src/lib/server/store.ts`) deletes the row, then `removePrefix`; if storage fails, the media stays forever with no row pointing at it. Deleted Clerk users (above) leave whole `users/<id>/` trees. Add a weekly reconciliation: list `users/*/projects/*` prefixes in storage, delete those with no project row. **S**
- **Finished uploads abandoned mid-way** are marked failed by `reconcile` after 30 minutes but `upload.bin` (and the S3 multipart upload, which bills) is never aborted. Call `abortUpload` from the sweeper. **S**
- **Frames folder grows unbounded**: `frames/<tenths>.jpg` are published per request and never pruned. Cap per project or regenerate on demand with a size limit. **S**

### 2.5 Security hardening

Nothing here is an open hole today; these are the gaps a security review of a product that fetches arbitrary URLs for users would flag.

- **SSRF via redirects and media URLs.** `checkPublicUrl` (`web/src/lib/server/media.ts`) resolves only the pasted hostname. yt-dlp follows redirects and extracts media URLs from any host, and ffmpeg then opens those (and HLS playlists that can reference anything). A page that redirects to `http://postgres:5432` or a cloud metadata address would be fetched. Fix at the network layer rather than in code: run the worker on a Docker network that can reach only Postgres and the internet, and add an egress rule in the container (or on the host) denying RFC1918, link-local and the metadata range for the worker's processes; alternatively route yt-dlp and ffmpeg through a small forward proxy that blocks private ranges (`--proxy` for yt-dlp, `http_proxy` for ffmpeg). **M**
- **Link lookups are a cheap way to exhaust the box.** `/api/sources/inspect` allows 30 per minute per user and each spawns a yt-dlp process (7 to 9 s, up to 30 MB of JSON). Add a global `limiter(4)` around `inspectUrl` like `withFrameLimit`, and a short negative cache for failures. **S**
- **Rate limits are per process and in memory** (`takeRateLimit` in `web/src/lib/server/http.ts`, documented). With a second web server each allows the full rate. Move the buckets to Postgres (one small table, `insert ... on conflict do update` with the window) or Redis when there is more than one web server. **S**
- **Missing headers.** `next.config.ts` sets nosniff, frame deny, referrer and permissions policies, but there is no `Strict-Transport-Security` (Caddy does not add it by default; add `header Strict-Transport-Security "max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains"` in `Caddyfile`) and no `Content-Security-Policy`. A CSP has to allow Clerk, Stripe Checkout redirects, the S3 public endpoint (signed media links) and inline styles; start with `report-only`. **S**
- **Upload chunks hold a row lock across network I/O.** `web/src/app/api/projects/[id]/upload/route.ts` calls `storage().uploadPart` inside `mutateProject`, so with S3 an 8 MB part is uploaded while a Postgres transaction and row lock are open (seconds per chunk, pool of 10). Upload the part first, then record it in the transaction (check the offset again inside; a duplicate part is harmless because S3 keeps the last ETag). **S**
- **YouTube cookies are a shared spare account.** One account's session for every user's YouTube import: YouTube rate-limits and bans it, and when it expires every YouTube import fails with "Try again later" until someone notices a log line. Alert on that log line (above), keep two or three cookie files and rotate on failure, and add yt-dlp's PO-token provider plugin or a residential proxy if volume grows. Make the fallback visible: when a link fails, the error already suggests uploading; add a one-click "download it yourself with this command" help for creators who own the video. Also pin yt-dlp to a tested version in `scripts/setup-media.mjs` (it fetches `releases/latest` today; a bad release would break every link import on the next build) and update it on a schedule with the e2e `E2E_LIVE=1` test as the gate. **S**
- **Dependency and secret scanning** in CI (`npm audit --omit=dev`, Dependabot or Renovate, gitleaks). **S**

## 3. Systemic changes the architecture needs for growth

The design is right; these are the places where the implementation will not scale past a few dozen concurrent users or a second machine.

1. **Separate web and worker, and make the worker the unit of scale.** Already supported by `BAMIO_WORKER=off` and `dist/worker.mjs`; production just does not use it. Then: storage to Cloudflare R2 (`STORAGE_DRIVER=s3`; egress is free, which matters for video), Postgres managed or at least on its own volume with WAL archiving, and N worker Droplets or a CPU-optimised one. The queue's `perUserMax` and priority already do the fair-share work. Add `BAMIO_*_SLOTS` tuned to cores, and a pool for "small" jobs (frames, lookups) so a long transcription never blocks a thumbnail. **M**
2. **Autoscale transcription on demand.** CPU transcription is the cost and the bottleneck. Options in order of effort: (a) a bigger always-on worker; (b) a second worker Droplet started by a small scheduler when `oldestWaitingSec` on `/api/health` passes a threshold and stopped when the queue drains (DigitalOcean API, image already built); (c) GPU for Omnilingual 1B (sherpa-onnx has CUDA builds; a T4-class GPU takes it from about 2x to 30x or more real time, which turns an hour of Hindi from an hour into two minutes). Do (a) now, (b) when there are paying users, (c) when non-English is a meaningful share. **M to L**
3. **Push progress instead of polling.** `useProject` polls every second while anything is busy, and each job writes progress to the project row every 750 ms (`jobWriter`). That is one project write and one browser read per second per active job; with 50 users editing while 50 imports run it is hundreds of queries a second for progress alone. Add a Server-Sent Events route per project that forwards Postgres `NOTIFY` on project changes (the queue already uses NOTIFY), keep polling as the fallback, and write progress to a small `job_progress` table (or the jobs row) instead of rewriting the whole project document. **M**
4. **Direct-to-storage uploads.** With S3/R2, let the browser PUT parts to presigned URLs instead of through Next.js (8 MB chunks through the web server today), and finish the multipart upload server-side. Halves web server load during uploads and removes the 10 MB body ceiling as a design constraint. **M**
5. **A project document that will outgrow one jsonb row.** Each project is one validated JSON blob rewritten on every change (clips up to 60, upload parts up to 10,000 ETags, job state). It is simple and correct, and it is why progress writes are expensive and why two users can't edit one project. Fine for now; when teams arrive (section 4), move clips to their own table with their own row locks and keep the project row for metadata. Note it so the migration is planned, not forced. **L, later**
6. **Feature flags and plan entitlements in one place.** Today a feature's availability is `aiConfigured()`, `billingEnabled()`, `allowance()` and `soon` flags spread across routes. Add an `entitlements(userId)` that returns what this user may do (minutes, projects, bytes, watermark, 4K, templates, team seats, API), fed by the plan, grants and a flags table, and read by routes and UI alike. Every feature in section 4 needs it. **M**
7. **A real staging and release process.** Tagged images, a `CHANGELOG.md`, migrations tested against a copy of production data before deploy, and `db-migrate` run as a separate step rather than on every container start (two containers starting at once already rely on the advisory lock; keep that, but make migration failures stop the deploy, not the app). **S**
8. **Health and admin for operators.** `/api/health` is good. Add to the admin overview: imports per day, median time per step (download, prepare, transcribe, find) by language, failures by error code, disk and queue trends, and the YouTube cookie state. Those numbers decide the sizing in item 2. **M**

## 4. What the best tool of its kind has, and Bamio does not yet

Benchmarked against what the category leaders (OpusClip, Klap, Vizard, Munch, Submagic, Descript, CapCut, and for streamers Eklipse and Powder) ship as standard in 2026. Bamio's `plans.ts` already lists most of these as coming soon; this is the order to build them in, judged by what sells and what Bamio's architecture makes easy.

### Tier A: table stakes (people compare on these before buying)

1. **Speaker-aware reframing (face tracking, "smart reframe").** The single biggest gap. `clipEditSchema` has one static `focusX` for the whole clip; `renderFilter` crops a fixed rectangle. A podcast with two hosts needs the crop to follow whoever speaks; a streamer needs a **split layout** (face cam on top, gameplay below) or picture-in-picture. Plan:
   - *Step 1, keyframed focus (M):* `focus: { t, x, y }[]` on the edit, a timeline of keyframes in the editor, `crop` with time expressions (or `sendcmd`) in the export, interpolated in the preview. Also adds `focusY` and a zoom factor, which 1:1 and 16:9 outputs need today.
   - *Step 2, automatic faces (L):* sample frames at 2 to 4 fps with ffmpeg, run a small face detector (YuNet or BlazeFace in ONNX through `onnxruntime-node`, which sherpa-onnx already bundles), group detections into shots, smooth the centre with a one-euro filter, and write the keyframes from step 1. Pick the active speaker by combining faces with speaker turns from diarization (Tier B item 2) or, until then, mouth movement and face size.
   - *Step 3, layouts (M):* "split" and "pip" framings for streams: detect the static face-cam rectangle (a region whose content changes while the rest changes differently, or let the user drag it once per project), and compose two crops.
2. **Caption styling.** Three fixed styles (`pop`, `clean`, `boxed`) with fixed colours in `web/src/lib/clips/ass.ts`. Leaders offer dozens of animated templates, keyword highlighting, emojis, per-word colour, font, size, position by drag, stroke and shadow controls, and uppercase. Build a caption *style object* (font family from a curated list of OFL fonts, size, colours for text, highlight, stroke, background, animation: pop, karaoke, slide, none, words per line, case, position as a y fraction), render it in the preview and the ASS the same way (the `markup` seam exists), ship 10 to 15 presets, and let Pro save custom presets as **brand kits** (with a logo overlay and a watermark position). This is also where "Basic templates" and "1 brand" on Starter come from. **L**
3. **Hooks, descriptions and hashtags.** Titles and reasons exist; the post needs a hook line for the first frame, a description and hashtags per platform. One more Gemini call per clip batch (cached transcript, a few hundred output tokens), an "AI title" button in the editor, and copy-to-clipboard on the project page. **S**
4. **Silence and filler-word removal.** The word-timed transcript makes this cheap: detect gaps over N ms and words from a filler list (um, uh, like, you know, per language), show them as cuts on the trim bar, and export with `select`/`aselect` filters or by concatenating segments (re-encode is already happening). Also "jump cuts" that tighten pacing, which is what makes clips feel professional. **M**
5. **Publishing and scheduling.** Export then upload by hand is where most tools win on time saved. OAuth to YouTube (Shorts), TikTok (Content Posting API), Instagram (Graph API, business accounts) and X; a "Post" button with title, description and hashtags prefilled; a schedule table and a job kind `publish` with retries; a content calendar on the project list. Each platform app needs review by the platform, which takes weeks: start the applications now. **L**
6. **Editor basics the pros expect:** undo and redo (there is none; the autosave `queue` makes a history stack easy), split and merge caption phrases (the editor can only edit words inside a phrase; `updateSegmentSchema` has no split), nudge a word's timing, caption font size per clip, a zoom slider, batch export of selected clips as a zip (`archiver` streaming into storage), per-clip aspect override on export ("export this clip in 9:16 and 1:1"), and keyboard shortcuts listed in a help sheet. **M**
7. **4K export** (sold on Pro and Team). `OUTPUT_SIZE` to 2160x3840 when the source is 4K; libx264 at that size on CPU is slow, so gate it to Pro and consider `-preset faster` or hardware encoders on a GPU worker. Also offer 720p for fast previews. **S**
8. **Loudness normalisation** (`loudnorm` to -14 LUFS, two-pass) and a music bed option (licensed library, ducked under speech) on export. Shorts platforms normalise anyway, but consistent levels across a batch is a quality signal. **S**

### Tier B: differentiators that fit what Bamio already does better than anyone

1. **Hype from the chat for live streams.** Bamio's hype signal is loudness. For Twitch and Kick (and YouTube live) the chat is the strongest signal there is: messages per second, emote bursts, "clip it" and "W" density. Record the chat alongside a followed stream (Twitch IRC is open; Kick has a chat websocket; YouTube live chat via the Data API), align it to the capture timeline, and feed `promptLines` a `chat` mark like the loudness marks. Also auto-mark candidate moments on the timeline while following, so a streamer's editor can clip live. No general-purpose clipper does this; the stream-focused ones do not transcribe every language on device. **M**
2. **Speaker diarization.** sherpa-onnx ships offline speaker diarization (segmentation plus speaker embeddings). Label speakers in the transcript, show them in captions as colour or name, use speaker turns for the reframing in Tier A item 1, and offer "clips where the guest speaks". Fulfils "Speaker detection" on Pro. **M**
3. **Punctuation and capitals for Omnilingual languages.** Captions in Hindi, Arabic, Japanese and the other 1,600 languages are lowercase and unpunctuated. Add a punctuation model (sherpa-onnx has CT-Transformer punctuation for Chinese and English; for others a small seq2seq or a cheap Gemini pass over the text only, keeping word times) and bump `TRANSCRIBER_VERSION`. **M**
4. **Translated captions and dubbing-free localisation.** Translate the word-timed transcript (Gemini, cached transcript) and burn in a second language, or export one clip in several languages. Natural for a product whose pitch is "every language". **M**
5. **"Clip anything" by prompt.** "Find every time they talk about pricing", "the funniest moments", "clips for a product launch". A prompt field on "Find more clips" that becomes part of `highlightPrompt`; the compact transcript and cache make each extra search cheap. **S**
6. **Chapters and long-form summaries** from the same transcript: a chapter list on the project page, a one-paragraph summary for the YouTube description, a "best moments" reel that stitches several clips with transitions. **M**
7. **Per-platform safe zones** in the preview (TikTok's right-hand icons and bottom caption area, Reels' bottom bar, Shorts' title area) as toggleable overlays, with caption position defaults that avoid them. **S**
8. **Accuracy loop.** Measure Gemini's picks against outcomes: once publishing exists, pull views per posted clip and correlate with score, length and position; use it to tune `hypeScore` weights and the prompt, per content type (podcast, gaming, talk). This is how "virality score" becomes a real number rather than a label. **M, after publishing**

### Tier C: growth and business features

1. **Teams and client workspaces** (Team plan): organisations in Clerk, projects owned by an org, roles (owner, editor, reviewer), a client approval link for a clip (view, comment, approve) and brand kits per client. This is the change that forces the project-document split in section 3 item 5. **L**
2. **Public API and webhooks** (Team): API keys, `POST /v1/imports`, `GET /v1/projects/:id`, `POST /v1/clips/:id/export`, a webhook on ready; the job system makes this mostly plumbing plus docs and rate limits per key. **M**
3. **Analytics** (Team): posted-clip performance per platform once publishing exists. **M**
4. **Integrations that bring videos in:** a browser extension that adds "Clip with Bamio" on YouTube, Twitch and Kick pages (and sidesteps data-centre blocking by downloading in the user's browser with their session), Google Drive and Dropbox import, Zoom and Riverside recordings, RSS for podcasts. **M each**
5. **Localised UI** (the product transcribes every language but its interface is English only). Extract strings, start with Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Japanese. **M**
6. **Mobile:** the editor is responsive; a PWA install prompt and a "share to Bamio" target on phones are small wins. **S**
7. **Marketplace and growth loops:** caption and brand templates shared by users, "Made with Bamio" watermark on free exports that links back, the referral programme extended to free users (credits instead of cash). **M**

## 5. Code-level findings (specific, from reading the code)

Beyond the items above, things a reviewer would flag now. None is a correctness bug in normal use; they are debts to settle while the codebase is still small.

| Where | Finding | Suggested change |
|---|---|---|
| `web/src/lib/server/jobs.ts` `runImport` | Scratch dir removed only on success; `analyze` and exports create their own dirs under it. | Sweeper (section 2.4); or `finally` cleanup when the job ends for good. |
| `web/src/lib/server/jobs.ts` `jobWriter.progress` | Fire-and-forget writes with `.catch(() => undefined)` rewrite the whole project document every 750 ms per job and can land after a later `set`. | Progress in its own column or table; the SSE change in section 3. |
| `web/src/app/api/projects/[id]/upload/route.ts` | Part upload inside the row-locked transaction. | Upload, then record (section 2.5). |
| `web/src/app/api/sources/inspect/route.ts`, `media.ts` `inspectUrl` | No global concurrency cap on yt-dlp lookups; 30 MB JSON buffered per call. | `limiter(4)`; stream-parse or `--flat-playlist`-style trimming of fields with `-O`/`--print` where possible. |
| `web/src/lib/server/media.ts` `checkPublicUrl` | Only the pasted host is resolved; redirects and media hosts are not. | Network egress rules (section 2.5). |
| `web/src/lib/server/http.ts` `takeRateLimit` | In-memory buckets per process. | Postgres or Redis buckets when a second web process exists. |
| `web/next.config.ts`, `web/Caddyfile` | No HSTS, no CSP. | Add both (CSP in report-only first). |
| `web/src/lib/server/store.ts` `deleteProject` | Row deleted before media; failures orphan media. | Delete media first with a tombstone status, or reconcile weekly. |
| `web/src/lib/server/store.ts` `reconcile` | Runs an `activeJobs` query on every read and list. Fine now; it is a second query per project in `listProjects`. | Already batched for lists; keep. Note for SSE work. |

## 6. Suggested order

Each phase ends with `npm run check`, `npm run build`, the e2e suite, and an update to `DESIGN_STATUS.md`, as today.

**Phase 1, make it safe to sell (2 to 3 weeks of focused work).** CI with the Docker build and e2e; images built in CI and pulled on the Droplet; split web and worker and size the worker; off-box backups and a tested restore; Sentry plus uptime alerts plus the four operational alerts; HSTS and CSP; sweepers for scratch, orphans and abandoned uploads; terms, privacy, takedown; account deletion via Clerk webhook; support address; analytics events; model attribution; inspect concurrency cap; yt-dlp pinned; cookie-expiry alert.

**Phase 2, make people start (1 to 2 weeks).** Free tier or trial with a watermark; sample project on first sign-in; welcome email; hooks, descriptions and hashtags; loudness normalisation; undo/redo and caption split/merge; batch export. Then launch posts, directories, and the SEO pages already built start to matter.

**Phase 3, close the feature gap (6 to 10 weeks).** Caption styling system with presets and brand kits (removes most `soon` flags on Starter and Pro); keyframed focus with zoom, then automatic face tracking, then split layouts for streamers; silence and filler removal; speaker diarization; 4K. Re-measure clip quality with real Gemini on real transcripts after each prompt change, as the CLAUDE.md rule says.

**Phase 4, own the stream niche and publishing (6 to 8 weeks).** Chat-based hype for Twitch, Kick and YouTube live; live moment markers while following; reconnect after a dropped connection; publishing to YouTube, TikTok and Instagram with scheduling (platform app reviews started in Phase 1); the accuracy loop.

**Phase 5, scale and business (ongoing).** SSE, direct uploads, R2, managed Postgres, autoscaled or GPU workers, entitlements and flags, teams and client approval, API, analytics, localisation, extension.

## 7. What to measure from day one

- Funnel: visitors, sign-ups, first import started, first import ready, first export, first checkout, paid; by source.
- Reliability: import success rate by platform and language; median and p95 time per step by language and video length; export success rate; failed jobs by error code; `/api/health` uptime.
- Cost: CPU minutes per AI minute sold, by language; Gemini tokens per video; storage bytes per user; egress.
- Quality: share of AI clips exported or posted (a proxy for "the pick was good"), clips edited before export (trim distance from the AI's cut), caption edits per minute of video by language.
- Money: MRR by plan (admin shows it), churn, referral conversions, minutes used versus bought.

Bamio's foundations are unusually good for its age. The work above is mostly the unglamorous part (operations, legal, free tier, the feature set the category treats as table stakes) plus three bets that only Bamio can make cheaply: every language, live streams, and hype from the chat.
